The migration landed in April and cut p ninety-nine latency by 62 percent while throughput more than quadrupled.

It shipped at three forty-seven in the morning UTC on April ninth, roughly 18 months after the original RFC, and it touched about 1,240 files across 7 packages. Throughput went from around 450 requests per second to 2,100 requests per second, p ninety-nine latency dropped 62 percent, from 890 milliseconds to 338 milliseconds, and the AWS bill fell by 14,500 dollars a month, which honestly nobody expected.

The tricky part wasn't the SQL. It was that PostgreSQL version 16.2 changed how nulls distinct behaves in unique indexes, so about 0.3 percent of rows that had quietly coexisted for years suddenly collided. We caught it in staging thankfully, rolled back within 11 minutes, added a dedupe pass, and re-ran.

If you're doing something similar, read the release notes twice, keep the old read replica warm for at least 48 hours, and don't schedule a cutover on a Friday. Ours ran Thursday, so we still had a full workday to fix things, versus the alternative, which is debugging a 500 error storm at two in the morning on a Saturday while your on-call engineer's phone is on do not disturb.
