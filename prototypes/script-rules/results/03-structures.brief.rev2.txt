No, you can't safely share one connection pool across forked workers.

When you fork, the system duplicates file descriptors but not the protocol state, so two workers end up interleaving bytes on the same wire. There are three things that go wrong, starting with the most common. First, interleaved protocol frames: worker A sends a query, worker B sends another, and both read whichever response arrives first, so you get worker A's rows returned to worker B. Symptoms include "column does not exist" on a query that definitely has that column, or occasional "unexpected message type" errors from the driver. Second is double-close, where whichever worker exits first tears down the socket and the others see connection reset. And third is silent pool starvation: the parent thinks it holds n connections, but the OS sees n times the worker count descriptors pointing at n sockets, and the server's max connections accounting disagrees with everyone.

The fix is to create the pool after the fork, sized so the total stays under budget. The sizing rule of thumb is to take max connections times 0.8, divide by worker count, and round down. With max connections at 100 and 4 workers, that's 20 each; round down to 18 so you have headroom when a worker is mid-restart and its old sockets haven't timed out yet. Leave at least 5 connections free for superuser access and migrations. One caveat: if you're behind PgBouncer in transaction mode, the bouncer owns the real connections and your pool is just a set of cheap client-side handles, so oversizing the app pool is nearly free and max connections on Postgres itself can stay small.
