Throughput went from about 450 requests per second to 2,100 requests per second. P ninety-nine latency dropped 62 percent, from 890 milliseconds to 338 milliseconds. The AWS bill fell by 14,500 dollars a month, which honestly nobody expected. The tricky part wasn't the SQL; it was that PostgreSQL version 16.2 changed how NULLS DISTINCT behaves in unique indexes, so about 0.3 percent of rows that had quietly coexisted for years suddenly collided.

We caught it in staging, thankfully, rolled back within 11 minutes, added a dedupe pass, and re-ran. If you're doing something similar, read the release notes twice and keep the old read-replica warm for at least 48 hours. Don't schedule a cutover on a Friday.

Ours ran Thursday, so we still had a full workday to fix things versus the alternative, which is debugging a 500-error storm at 2 a.m. on a Saturday while your on-call engineer's phone is on Do Not Disturb.
